# Eye-Tracking Productivity Monitor

For my Discovery Project, I built an application that uses a laptop camera to track a user’s eyes and attention in real time. The purpose of the project was to detect when the user became distracted, especially by looking at their phone or repeatedly looking away from their screen, and then respond with a loud alert sound. I wanted to create something practical that could help people stay focused while studying or working, while also giving me the chance to explore computer vision and real-time software design.

The original idea for the project came from a common problem: procrastination during work sessions. It is easy to lose focus by checking a phone for a few seconds, which can quickly turn into a larger distraction. My goal was to design a system that could recognize this kind of off-task behavior and immediately notify the user. Instead of relying on self-discipline alone, the application acts as a real-time reminder to stay engaged.

Throughout the semester, I worked on building the project step by step. I first focused on capturing video from the laptop camera and making sure the application could continuously process live input. After that, I worked on detecting the user’s face and eyes so the program could estimate whether the user was looking at the screen or away from it. Once that part was working, I added the notification system that plays a loud sound when distraction is detected. I also spent time adjusting the detection logic so that normal movement would not trigger the sound too often.

One of the biggest successes of the project was getting a working prototype that could respond to live behavior in real time. At the same time, I faced several challenges. Eye tracking is highly sensitive to lighting, camera quality, head movement, and user position, so reliability was difficult to maintain. Another challenge was that procrastination is hard to define precisely. Looking away from a screen does not always mean a person is off task, so I had to simplify the problem into something the software could realistically detect.

This project helped me build important ECE-related skills, especially in programming, debugging, system integration, and computer vision concepts. It also taught me how to think more carefully about human-centered design and the limitations of real-world systems. Overall, this project was a valuable experience because it combined technical problem-solving with a practical everyday issue, and it increased my interest in intelligent software systems.
